# Chapter Planning and Memo

## Shared rules

Treat author intent, fictional world rules, narrative design and runtime governance as distinct responsibilities. Ground story constraints and character behavior in the frozen author brief or selected work design. Candidate status, host identity/permission checks, approval, validation, receipts, retries and evidence completeness govern this generation; they are not fictional events or character motives. Knowledge boundaries restrict what a character may know; they do not require the character to ask, verify or obtain permission in every scene. Procedural action is valid when this work supplies its reason. Quiet observation and stable situations are valid when selected by the author or design. If an inherited plan conflicts with that intent, expose the structural conflict for the existing author/Plan Change decision; do not hide it by polishing the same scenes.

Use author intent, relevant character and world state, accepted events and due obligations. Separate proposed future action from accepted facts. Select commercial and craft techniques for the chapter's function, rather than requiring a reward, reversal or cost in every scene. Return the declared JSON result; the host owns version binding, validation and activation.

## Chapter planning

Translate the frozen scheduling row into a causal scene sequence. Give each scene a concrete situation and the relevant actors or viewpoint. Use objectives, obstacles and change where the selected purpose needs them; sustained observation or an unchanged situation can be intentional. Connect entry and exit states. Establish what the POV actor knows before acting and what the reader can learn during the scene. Preserve the exact scheduling fields and identity references required by the task; elaborate only the unscheduled detail. Make the title and synopsis reader-facing.

## Memo

Turn the frozen mission into useful scene and voice guidance. Identify the immediate dramatic question, character motive, useful detail and a plausible transition where they help this chapter. Refer to Plan facts without copying the entire Plan or grading the future prose. Suggest only techniques supported by this scene's purpose; leave an optional direction absent when it adds no value. Retrieval queries should seek missing relevant story facts, not prior quality scores or development records.

## World transition proposals

Propose bounded world developments for the frozen Chapter planning task using its Foundation, current Reality and desired transformation. Distinguish hard prerequisites, optional preparation, costs, consequences and information scope. Preserve accepted cause references and local proposal dependencies. Off-camera activity does not grant reader or character knowledge. Offer NO_OP or DEFER when change would not serve the current situation; proposals do not establish completed events.

## World transition evaluation

Assess each exact supplied transition and preparation path against the frozen Director dimensions, hard prerequisites and information boundary. Explain its consequences for this Chapter's dramatic purpose. Distinguish promising optional developments from executable ones and compare only supplied alternatives. Do not invent missing causes or relax a prerequisite to raise a score.

## Actor intention

Act only within the current actor's supplied knowledge, motives, resources and permitted action space. Propose a plausible intention, preparation or response to the perceived situation; distinguish belief from fact and a plan from a completed action. Preserve secrecy and avoid using another actor's private knowledge. The Director and owning Authority decide execution and state changes.
