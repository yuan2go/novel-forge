# Topic Development

## Shared rules

Treat author intent, fictional world rules, narrative design and runtime governance as distinct responsibilities. Ground story constraints and character behavior in the frozen author brief or selected work design. Candidate status, host identity/permission checks, approval, validation, receipts, retries and evidence completeness govern this generation; they are not fictional events or character motives. Knowledge boundaries restrict what a character may know; they do not require the character to ask, verify or obtain permission in every scene. Procedural action is valid when this work supplies its reason. Quiet observation and stable situations are valid when selected by the author or design. If an inherited plan conflicts with that intent, expose the structural conflict for the existing author/Plan Change decision; do not hide it by polishing the same scenes.

Use the author's desired experience, genre, intended audience and boundaries as the starting point. Abstract reference mechanisms may inform an original design; protected expression, distinctive character combinations and copied scene sequences may not. Approved parents are design conditions, not accepted chapter events. Return the declared candidate schema; the host supplies lineage and approval.

## Creative direction

Build a specific premise from the people, situation and experience selected by the author. Use goals and resistance where that narrative purpose calls for them. Make the attraction and differentiation concrete through actions, relationships or a discoverable question. Choose the scale and emotional texture appropriate to the author's brief. A story need not adopt escalation, power growth or a commercial hook schedule unless the brief calls for them. Produce the requested distinct candidates with meaningful tradeoffs.

## Reader promise

Create the promise this new story makes to its intended reader using the frozen creative, theme and ending inputs. State the expected emotional or dramatic experience, the recurring source of anticipation, what later developments must earn, and what would betray that promise. Use the requested typed promise fields and identities. This is a new-story design task, not an analysis of a reference work or a market-performance report. Do not invent a source-analysis path or claim observed reader approval.
