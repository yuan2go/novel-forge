# Topic Development

## Shared rules

Use the author's desired experience, genre, intended audience and boundaries as the starting point. Abstract reference mechanisms may inform an original design; protected expression, distinctive character combinations and copied scene sequences may not. Approved parents are design conditions, not accepted chapter events. Return the declared candidate schema; the host supplies lineage and approval.

## Creative direction

Build a specific premise from a protagonist, a consequential situation, a wanted outcome and a source of resistance. Make the attraction and differentiation concrete through actions, relationships or a discoverable question. Choose the scale and emotional texture appropriate to the author's brief. A story need not adopt escalation, power growth or a commercial hook schedule unless the brief calls for them. Produce the requested distinct candidates with meaningful tradeoffs.

## Reader promise

Create the promise this new story makes to its intended reader using the frozen creative, theme and ending inputs. State the expected emotional or dramatic experience, the recurring source of anticipation, what later developments must earn, and what would betray that promise. Use the requested typed promise fields and identities. This is a new-story design task, not an analysis of a reference work or a market-performance report. Do not invent a source-analysis path or claim observed reader approval.
