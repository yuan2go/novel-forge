# Long Outline

## Shared rules

Translate the author's intent, theme, character desires, world limits and reader promise into causal developments at the requested scale. Proposed events remain future design. Preserve frozen parent lineage, accepted boundaries, ranges and obligations. Ground concepts, relationships and clues before later action depends on them. Commercial escalation, rewards and cliffhangers are options governed by this story's intent, not universal requirements. Return the declared JSON candidate; the host validates and activates plans.

## Ending intent

Design the intended resolution of the central conflict and thematic question. Connect the ending to consequential character choices and the reader promise. Specify what changes, what remains unresolved and what must be prepared earlier. Preserve approved theme and creative conditions without pretending future events are accepted facts.

## Book planning

Lay out the main causal path, volume functions, major transformations and setup/payoff windows within the frozen length. Allocate room for consequences and character development. Each declared promise or mystery window must use its exact supplied identity and a feasible setup and payoff range. Avoid turning the book into a list of unrelated climaxes or an encyclopedic chronology.

## Volume planning

Give this volume a purpose in the parent Book: its entry situation, central pressure, relationships, developments and exit transformation. Detail only this volume's allocated scope. Payoffs and further pressure follow the story promise and frozen schedule; every volume need not end with a larger enemy or power upgrade.

## Arc planning

Trace the specified character, relationship, conflict or mystery movement through ordered segments. Connect each segment's entry to its predecessor's exit and prepare its later consequences. Use relations between distinct Arc identities for cross-arc dependencies, not self-edges to order one Arc's segments.

## Chapter-group planning

Within the exact frozen planning horizon, make an ordered chapter-function matrix and a causal event chain covering every chapter. Use the branch strategy to realize the desired transformation while preserving hard constraints and accepted boundaries. Vary chapter functions where the story needs contrast or breathing room. Schedule only permitted disclosures and explain how developments prepare later action.
