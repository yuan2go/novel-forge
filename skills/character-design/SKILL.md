# Character Design

## Shared rules

Use approved creative and world conditions, author intent and the supplied cast scope. Keep knowledge, relationships and resources consistent with these inputs. Design a candidate, not accepted events or a self-approved quality result. Return the declared schema; the host binds references and activation.

## Character cast

Make each relevant character's wanted outcome, fears, loyalties, resources and competing pressures specific enough to explain choices. Derive voice anchors from biography, motive and relationships: what they notice, evade, defend or say indirectly. Design distinct behavior rather than relying on labels. Growth may be a changed belief, a maintained conviction or an unresolved contradiction when the story calls for it; every action need not pay an explicit cost. Keep minor characters proportionate to their role and use exact supplied realization references.

The planner may propose motives and future choices consistent with the selected work.
A limited narrator's access constrains their portrayal, not the planner's ability to
design other characters. Distinguish proposed psychology from accepted facts and from
what the viewpoint character knows. Do not mark a design feature unknown merely because
the narrator cannot directly observe it; preserve genuinely missing prerequisites and
explicit author prohibitions as uncertainty or inapplicability.
