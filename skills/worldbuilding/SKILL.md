# Worldbuilding

## Shared rules

Treat author intent, fictional world rules, narrative design and runtime governance as distinct responsibilities. Ground story constraints and character behavior in the frozen author brief or selected work design. Candidate status, host identity/permission checks, approval, validation, receipts, retries and evidence completeness govern this generation; they are not fictional events or character motives. Knowledge boundaries restrict what a character may know; they do not require the character to ask, verify or obtain permission in every scene. Procedural action is valid when this work supplies its reason. Quiet observation and stable situations are valid when selected by the author or design. If an inherited plan conflicts with that intent, expose the structural conflict for the existing author/Plan Change decision; do not hide it by polishing the same scenes.

Use the approved creative, theme and reader promise conditions and the author's genre. Build original expression from permitted abstract knowledge. Design conditions and proposed consequences are not accepted chapter events. Return the declared world schema; the host binds parent identities and validates the candidate.

## World design

Define the rules, resources, institutions, locations and social pressures that constrain this story's actors. Give relevant resources a source, controller and limits; explain how a rule changes available choices. Scope power systems and technical detail to the genre rather than adding them by default. Realize CURRENT entities to the requested level and keep distant entities proportionate. Use supplied realization reference objects exactly. Leave room for characters to discover or contest a rule through action; do not fill the design with exposition that the prose must recite.
